# QuadLoom 0.4.6 — better quads, faster, and Symmetry put right

The first public release since 0.4.0. Versions 0.4.1 to 0.4.5 were built and tested but not released on their own; everything in them is in 0.4.6. Their notes are kept in this folder ([0.4.1](0.4.1.md), [0.4.2](0.4.2.md), [0.4.3](0.4.3.md), [0.4.4](0.4.4.md), [0.4.5](0.4.5.md)).

## What is new since 0.4.0

- **The quad stage is rebuilt** (0.4.1, decision [0023](../decisions/0023-quad-geometry.md)). Pattern points are placed by solving, a new relaxer keeps thin parts and lets feature vertices slide along their lines, long uneven patches get a bridged grid instead of a fan, and patches that cannot be filled safely keep their triangles instead of failing the run.
- **The worker no longer fails on `progress.json`** (0.4.1, decision [0021](../decisions/0021-crash-containment.md), addendum). 0.4.0 failed about one run in six on Windows with `Permission denied ... progress.json`.
- **Each part gets its own size** (0.4.2, decision [0024](../decisions/0024-size-per-part-strip-relaxing.md)). Flat, open and many-part models no longer get runaway quad counts, and parts with close feature lines get finer quads. Thin strips between feature lines no longer lean.
- **New setting: Ignore Faint Creases** (0.4.2; under Sharp Edges, off by default). Short or shallow crease lines stop steering the edge flow, for straighter flow on sculpts with fur, pores or scan noise. Right-angle edges, marked sharp edges, seams and borders are always kept.
- **Detect Settings** (0.4.3, decision [0025](../decisions/0025-detect-settings.md)). A button that reads the active mesh, shows what kind of model it is, suggests Sharp Edges and Ignore Faint Creases settings for it, and applies them on request. From 0.4.6 it also finds mirror planes and suggests Symmetry.
- **Thin parts keep their shape** (0.4.4, decision [0026](../decisions/0026-thin-parts.md)). A tail, finger or wire gets enough triangles round it to be laid out, small holes stay open, and a thin part gets at least six quads round it at any Density.
- **Layout Attempts** (0.4.5, decision [0027](../decisions/0027-best-of-n-layouts.md); main panel, 1 to 8, default 4). Each connected part is laid out several times, and each keeps the attempt whose quads score best on shape, irregular vertices and distance from the input. The result is still the same on every run: an attempt is a seed, not a random draw. Layout Attempts 1 gives the single-layout result.

## What is new in 0.4.6

Decision [0028](../decisions/0028-speed-and-symmetry.md).

**Speed.** There are no new settings for this.

- **About 30% less time overall.** The 70 models take 683 s through the add-on, where 0.4.5 took 964 s. The heavy models gain most: CommercialRefrigerator 73 s to 11 s, ChronographWatch 136 s to 80 s, AntiqueCamera 45 s to 15 s, the armadillo 28 s to 19 s. The median model takes about what it did.
- **The quad-count solve runs per part.** It solves each connected part of a model on its own, side by side, with a third of the rows it had. Models of many parts used to be too large for its full search and got only a local repair.
- **Relaxing no longer stalls on large hard-surface models.** It found nearest points with a search that had no distance bound until it found something. The fix gives the same result to the bit.
- **Inputs made of long thin triangles** (ruled surfaces, some CAD exports) no longer slow the first remesh: `crane/oloid` 38 s to 10 s.

**Symmetry** (main panel: X, Y, Z or Auto Detect; still off by default).

- **Density no longer doubles with Symmetry on.** One side used to be sized as if it were the whole model, so a mirrored result had nearly twice the quads. A symmetric head now gets 11,800 quads with Symmetry and 11,400 without, in less than half the time.
- **No more zero-size quads along the mirror plane** on a model that is not centred on its origin.
- **Auto Detect skips models with something on one side only** (a watch's crown, a curled tail). It used to mirror them, so the detail came out twice or not at all. The Tolerance setting under Advanced (default 0.5% of the object's size) is now the most any one point may differ from its mirror image; it used to be an average. The result line names the axis that was skipped and by how much. An axis you pick yourself is still mirrored as asked, with a warning.
- **Detect Settings now looks for mirror planes too.** It shows the planes it found and how much the two sides differ, and suggests Auto Detect when the model has one. Apply Suggested Settings turns it on. It never suggests turning Symmetry off.
- **The command-line tool** gains `quadloom run --symmetry <json>`.

## What to expect

- **Quality at the default settings is what 0.4.5's was.** Two runs of each build were compared, the second with a change that should mean nothing. Counting only models where both new runs beat or lose to both old ones, mean scaled Jacobian is clearly better on 10 models and worse on 11; chance alone gives about 12 either way. The models with the most parts gain, for example SunglassesKhronos (0.87 to 0.92) and ChronographWatch (0.77 to 0.79).
- **With Symmetry, on the 34 benchmark models that have a mirror plane:** a third of the time, the same quad shape, and an exactly symmetric result. It is a little farther from the input on about half of them (none over 1%). A face lying in the mirror plane itself is left out, and a flaw on the remeshed side appears on both sides.
- **The quad count differs from 0.4.0's**, mostly lower on models that used to run away, and moves a little with the layout chosen.

## Known limits

- **Windows x64 and Blender 5.2 only** are built and tested. The manifest permits Blender 4.2 and later.
- **Target Quads is approximate.** Coarse requests can miss substantially.
- **A connected part with more than 2,500 free patch sides** gets only the local repair of its quad counts.
- **An inverted quad** still appears in about 40 of the 70 benchmark results; it was 65 in 0.4.0.
- **Mirror planes** must lie along the object's own axes, through its origin or the middle of its bounding box.
- Shape keys and edge attributes (crease and bevel weights) are not transferred.

## Validation

Rust tests with all features and default, both clippy configurations, `cargo fmt --check`, the Python tests, and the headless Blender suite (51 scenarios) on the development build. The production zip then passed thirteen Blender smoke scenarios (remesh, Detect Settings, attributes, Quick Re-run, scene switch, malformed results, undo, the GUI remesh, Target Quads, presets, quads, Layout Attempts and Symmetry). Its backend reports version 0.4.6, API 13, fault injection off.
