# QuadLoom 0.4.5 — Layout Attempts

**Not released on its own.** 0.4.5 was built and tested but not published; its changes ship in [0.4.6](0.4.6.md), the first public release after 0.4.0. The artifact named below was never distributed.

## What is new (decision [0027](../decisions/0027-best-of-n-layouts.md))

- **Each part of a mesh is laid out several times and keeps its best quads.** A patch layout is one of many about as good as each other, and which one a run lands on decides how its quads come out: 0.4.4 showed that a change that means nothing gives most models another result. 0.4.5 makes that count for the user: every connected part gets four layouts by default, the quads of each are built and scored, and each part keeps the best.
- **A new setting, Layout Attempts** (main panel, under Adaptive Detail; 1 to 8, default 4). One is 0.4.4's result exactly. More attempts take longer and never rate worse. Presets leave it alone.
- **The result is still the same on every run.** An attempt is a seed, not a random draw: the same mesh and settings give the same quads.
- **Parts with nothing to choose are not built twice.** A box, a panel or a tube comes out the same from every seed, and only its tracing is repeated.
- The result line says what happened: "Best of 4 Layouts: 5 of 12 Parts Took Another". While they run, the status reads "Layout Attempts… 40%".
- It works with Target Count (the number asked for still holds), Symmetry and Quick Re-run (changing Layout Attempts reruns only the quad stage).
- Add-on API 12.

## What to expect

Over the 70 benchmark models at default settings, each build run twice (as built and with a change that means nothing), a model counted only when both new runs beat or lose to both old ones. About 12 of 70 would count either way by chance:

| Measure | Clearly better than 0.4.4 | Clearly worse | Median, 0.4.4 to 0.4.5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean scaled Jacobian | 29 | 3 | 0.941 to 0.954 |
| Scaled Jacobian, 5th percentile | 23 | 2 | 0.755 to 0.794 |
| Largest distance from the input | 24 | 1 | 0.355% to 0.315% of the diagonal |

- **The unlucky results are what goes.** The largest difference between two runs of one model is less than half what it was (worst quads: 0.54 to 0.20), and no result of either run is further than 1% from its input (0.4.4: one in each).
- **Models of many parts gain most**, since each part now has its own best layout: ABeautifulGame's worst quads go from 0.35 to 0.65, DamagedHelmet's from 0.45 to 0.69, ToyCar's from 0.32 to 0.42.
- **It takes about half as long again.** Through the add-on, eight models one at a time: 205 s in 0.4.4, 299 s in 0.4.5 (+46%). The later attempts run side by side, so four cost what three do, and they search for quad counts on a quarter of the solver effort so that one slow layout does not hold up the run. What a model pays depends on it: the armadillo 1.26 times its old time, the pig 1.86, the bunny 2.11.
- **The number of quads can differ from 0.4.4's**, both ways, where a part takes another layout: 0.9% more over all models, and on single models from 28% fewer to 39% more (a thin coil that gets seven quads round where it had six).
- Layout Attempts 1 gives 0.4.4's result and takes 0.4.4's time.

## Known limits

- **Attempts differ in the layout only.** A fault that is already in the prep mesh (the pig's tail on some inputs) is in every attempt.
- **A part that gets the same layout from every seed is not tried again**, though its quads could still have come out better (SunglassesKhronos' large frame).
- **"Best" is by quad shape, irregular vertices and distance from the surface.** Edge flow is not measured, so it is not picked for.
- An inverted quad still sits in about 40 of 70 results (0.4.4: 44).
- Up to four attempts are in memory at once.

## Through the add-on

All 70 benchmark models complete ([report](../../benchmark/reports/20261127-q045-default/README.md)). Against QRemeshify, over the 60 models it finishes: mean scaled Jacobian better on 53, its 5th percentile on 50, Hausdorff distance on 50 (0.4.4: 49, 47 and 46). Against 0.4.4's own run, model by model: mean scaled Jacobian better on 51 and worse on 3. One result is further than 1% from its input, the same one as in 0.4.4. The run takes 964 s of tool time against 0.4.4's 630 s, with two Blender processes at once where 0.4.4's had three.
